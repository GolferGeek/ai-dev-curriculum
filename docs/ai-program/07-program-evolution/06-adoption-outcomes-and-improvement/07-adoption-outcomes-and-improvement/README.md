# Adoption, outcomes, and improvement

This node defines how the organization distinguishes access, activity,
effective use, trusted outcomes, and sustained improvement. Adoption is not a
goal by itself: approved non-use may be the correct result for unsuitable,
restricted, or low-value work.

The company stance should define baselines, intended outcomes, leading and
lagging indicators, comparison groups where appropriate, cost and burden,
decision thresholds, review cadence, and who may stop, change, scale, or retire
an intervention.

## Balanced evidence

- setup, guided use, independent effectiveness, contribution, and coaching;
- cycle time, review time, rework, defects, incidents, and recovery;
- comprehension, verification quality, and retained human expertise;
- cost, latency, accessibility, support demand, and opportunity cost;
- confidence, frustration, trust, pressure, and escalation evidence; and
- customer, business, workforce, security, and compliance outcomes.

Metrics should identify distribution and unintended consequences rather than
reporting only organization-wide averages. Correlation with AI activity does
not establish causation; claims should state the comparison, period,
limitations, and confidence.

## Ask this node

- Are people merely using AI, or producing more trusted outcomes?
- Which teams, roles, or populations benefit or carry additional burden?
- Are quality, comprehension, and resilience changing with speed or cost?
- What evidence would cause us to revise, restrict, scale, or retire the work?

## Findings and review triggers

Report seat, prompt, or generated-line counts presented as value; missing
baselines; cherry-picked benefits; unmeasured review or support burden;
aggregate gains hiding harmed groups; claims without reproducible evidence; or
pilots that continue without a decision. Re-review monthly and after a material
quality, cost, workforce, customer, security, or compliance signal.
