# Psychological safety and escalation

This node defines how people can question AI output, disclose mistakes, stop
unsafe work, decline inappropriate AI use, and raise concerns without
retaliation. A technically correct escalation path is ineffective when people
do not trust it or when delivery incentives punish its use.

The company stance should name ordinary and confidential channels, emergency
stop paths, response times, independent review options, anti-retaliation
protections, documentation boundaries, and how reporters learn what happened.
Anonymous or confidential reporting must not promise protections the system
cannot actually provide.

## Required records

- challenge, stop-work, incident, ethics, security, and confidential channels;
- triage owners, service levels, independence, and escalation thresholds;
- anti-retaliation and conflict-of-interest controls;
- aggregate response and closure evidence; and
- recovery and learning process that avoids blaming good-faith reporters.

## Ask this node

- Can someone safely challenge this AI-assisted decision?
- Who can stop the work, and how quickly must the organization respond?
- Is there an independent path when the normal owner has a conflict?
- What evidence shows that concerns are resolved rather than merely received?

## Findings and review triggers

Report unknown or untrusted channels, unresolved reports, retaliation concerns,
conflicted investigators, pressure to conceal AI involvement, or a gap between
published and observed response. Re-review after any retaliation allegation,
material incident, repeated escalation, leadership change, response-time
failure, or evidence that people remain silent despite known problems.
