# AI culture, adoption, sentiment, and outcomes

This node governs how people experience, understand, challenge, adopt, decline,
and improve the organization's use of AI. Culture is the durable operating
environment; adoption is demonstrated behavior; sentiment is one evidence
source; outcomes show whether the program creates trusted value.

It is not an employee-surveillance program, a communications campaign, or a
scoreboard of prompt volume. A healthy AI culture allows people to question an
AI result, report harm or pressure, choose an approved non-AI path, learn from
failure, and understand how AI changes their work.

## Roles

| Role | Accountability |
|---|---|
| Executive sponsor | Outcomes, resources, risk posture |
| AI program owner | Program coherence, owners, cadence, escalation |
| Developer practice lead | Coding workflow, training, quality evidence |
| Security/data owner | Data, provider, authority, and incident decisions |
| Capability steward | Canonical skills/agents, publication, lifecycle |
| Model/tool evaluator | Workload tests, routing recommendations, watchlist |
| Coaches/champions | Local help, feedback, examples |
| Developers | Responsible use, verification, escalation, improvement proposals |

One person may hold several roles in a small company, but the accountabilities
must remain explicit.

## Adoption ladder

```text
aware → set up → guided use → independently effective
→ contributes improvements → coaches others
```

Do not measure adoption only by seats, prompts, or generated lines.

## Outcome measures

Balance:

- cycle time and time to first verified slice;
- review time, rework, escaped defects, incidents, and rollback;
- developer comprehension and ability to explain changes;
- test and evidence quality;
- model/tool cost and latency;
- policy exceptions and security findings;
- capability trigger precision, use, and retirement;
- developer confidence, frustration, and support demand; and
- business/customer outcomes appropriate to the product.

No single measure represents culture. Quantitative and qualitative evidence
must be interpreted together, with sampling limits, privacy boundaries, and
differences between teams or roles made explicit.

## GRC connections

- Direction and governance approves the desired culture, sponsor, scope, and
  decision rights.
- Risk management covers workforce harm, overreliance, deskilling, burnout,
  exclusion, retaliation, privacy, and unsafe pressure to use AI.
- Compliance and obligations records approved employment, accessibility,
  monitoring, consultation, and worker-representation requirements.
- Controls and assurance governs consent, access, retention, aggregation,
  escalation, training evidence, and independent review.
- Program intelligence may summarize approved aggregate evidence, but it must
  disclose uncertainty and must not infer individual attitude or performance.

## Operating rituals

- Monthly program health and terrain review.
- Monthly model/tool and capability portfolio checks.
- Quarterly policy and owner recertification.
- After-action review for incidents and meaningful failed experiments.
- Bounded pilots with explicit pass, fail, migration, and rollback.
- Regular office hours and examples drawn from real work.

## Ask this node

- Who owns this decision and who can approve an exception?
- Which teams need training or support?
- Are faster builds producing trusted outcomes?
- Which capabilities are useful, unused, conflicting, or ownerless?
- What should we pilot, integrate, defer, or retire?
- Which program document is stale?
- Do people feel able to challenge, refuse, or escalate AI-assisted work?
- What does approved sentiment evidence show, and what can it not show?
- Are role impacts, training needs, and uneven burdens being addressed?

## Findings

Report missing cultural principles, unclear workforce-impact ownership,
training without outcome evidence, low-trust escalation channels, pressure to
use AI regardless of fit, sentiment collection without an approved privacy
boundary, small-cohort re-identification risk, activity-only adoption metrics,
or material differences between leadership claims and workforce evidence.

## Review triggers

Re-review on ownership changes, adoption stalls, cost anomalies, quality
regression, major incidents, reorganizations, layoffs or material role change,
new monitoring or survey tools, repeated exceptions, retaliation concerns, or
evidence that the program optimizes activity rather than trusted outcomes.
