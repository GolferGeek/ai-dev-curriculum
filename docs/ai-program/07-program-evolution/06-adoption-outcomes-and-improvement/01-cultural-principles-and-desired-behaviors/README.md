# Cultural principles and desired behaviors

This node defines the human behaviors the organization wants around AI. It
turns broad values into observable expectations for leaders, developers,
reviewers, operators, and people whose work is affected by AI-assisted systems.

The company stance should address responsible experimentation, verification,
transparency, human accountability, respectful challenge, learning from
failure, appropriate non-use, knowledge sharing, and escalation. Leaders must
model the same boundaries expected of everyone else; speed or enthusiasm does
not excuse bypassing data, authority, quality, or evidence requirements.

## Required records

- approved cultural principles and prohibited behaviors;
- examples of desired planning, building, reviewing, and operating habits;
- leader commitments and consequences for unsafe pressure or concealment;
- relationship to performance management and recognition; and
- links to applicable conduct, ethics, security, and engineering policies.

## Ask this node

- What behaviors should AI use strengthen rather than erode?
- Is it acceptable to choose a non-AI path for this work?
- How should someone disclose and verify material AI assistance?
- Which incentives contradict the stated culture?

## Findings and review triggers

Report principles that are slogans without examples or accountability,
incentives that reward output volume over trusted outcomes, leaders operating
outside stated controls, or teams that cannot explain when non-use is correct.
Re-review after strategy or leadership change, a material incident, repeated
quality failure, workforce feedback, or evidence of unsafe local norms.
