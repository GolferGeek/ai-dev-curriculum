# Workforce experience and role impacts

This node governs how the organization identifies and responds to changes in
work, roles, workload, skill requirements, autonomy, accessibility, and career
paths caused by AI adoption. Efficiency claims are incomplete when the program
cannot explain who receives the benefit, who absorbs new review or cleanup
work, and which responsibilities remain human.

The company stance should define who assesses role impacts, how affected people
participate, which decisions require human-resources, legal, accessibility, or
worker-representation review, and how the organization addresses displacement,
deskilling, workload transfer, exclusion, or inequitable access to training.

## Required records

- affected roles, tasks, populations, locations, and employment arrangements;
- baseline and observed changes in workload, autonomy, quality, and support;
- retained human responsibilities and changed competency expectations;
- accessibility and equitable-access assessment;
- transition, training, redeployment, or support commitments; and
- approved consultation and escalation routes.

## Ask this node

- Whose work changes, and who was consulted?
- Which new review, correction, or evidence burden has been created?
- Are people losing necessary expertise or meaningful decision authority?
- Which impacts require a formal organizational or legal decision?

## Findings and review triggers

Report unassessed role changes, hidden labor transfer, inaccessible tools,
unequal training access, productivity gains paired with unsustainable review
load, or material impact decisions without authorized review. Re-review after
reorganization, job redesign, automation expansion, workforce reduction,
accessibility finding, grievance, or evidence of uneven outcomes.
