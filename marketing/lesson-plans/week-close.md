# Week Close (Day 5) — Lesson Plan

*The closing day introduces no new phase. Its job is to convert the week into the participants' own operating model — the course's actual business deliverable. It pairs with the [adoption kit](../adoption-kit/README.md) (the seven fill-in templates) and the D-CLOSE talks in [discussion-topics.md](discussion-topics.md).*

**Session arc:** Opening synthesis → Adoption-kit working session → Final project → Presentations → Send-off. Unlike Phases 00–06, the "build" today is *their* project and *their* program — the instructor coaches rather than lectures.

---

## 1. Opening synthesis — "What the week actually taught" (~30 min)

Walk the arc backwards so the shape is visible:

- **Phase 00–01:** the pipeline, and proof that "real" (auth + per-user data) is where trust is earned.
- **Phase 02–03:** generation is cheap; **review and comprehension** are the scarce skills — for new code and old.
- **Phase 04–05:** where it's going — agents that coordinate, and organizational knowledge packaged so agents can use it.
- **Phase 06:** stop guessing about models; measure against *your* work.

Land the closing thesis: **the tools on screen this week will be different in a year; the discipline — define well, verify hard, encode what you learn, keep humans accountable — is what they're taking home.** Then say plainly what "taking it home" means: a filled-in adoption kit and a first safe target, not a memory of a fast demo.

## 2. Adoption-kit working session (D-CLOSE-1, ~60–90 min)

Teams open the [adoption kit](../adoption-kit/README.md) and fill in all seven templates with their own names, tools, and owners:

1. Harness + plan tier decision
2. Instruction passport location + its first ten lines
3. Artifact folder convention
4. Gate commands they'll actually run
5. Decision-boundary one-pager
6. Routing + spend policy (manual is fine for now)
7. First brownfield target + Day-2 safety card

**Room mechanics:** work template-by-template, ~10 minutes each, instructor circulating. After each, one team reads a line aloud — hearing another shop's answer is half the value. Every template must leave with an **owner's name** in it; an unowned piece is the one that dies.

Weave in the two short closers while teams work: **D-CLOSE-2** (the same discipline works for ops, marketing, and finance intentions — who outside engineering gets a seat?) and **D-CLOSE-3** (specs, rules, preference skills, and evals compound; model brand loyalty and chat memories expire).

## 3. Final project (~2.5–3 hrs)

Each participant or team picks one:

- Extend or harden an in-week app (a Phase 01–05 slice).
- Finish or demo Phase 04 / 05 / 06 capstone work.
- Prototype a customer-facing tool or new product idea with the full pipeline.
- Analyze and improve a small or sanitized company codebase (private-cohort default).
- Ship a team AI-development checklist plus a live demo of the workflow.

The instructor's job during the run: enforce the pipeline (intention first, even ten lines), keep predict-then-compare alive, and push every team to verify in the browser before calling anything done.

### Private cohort variant

Day 5 becomes a supervised **their-repo** day: repository orientation, local startup, tool configuration and permission boundaries, first low-risk AI-assisted tasks, and a team checklist for reviewing agent output. The adoption-kit session still happens — templates 2, 5, and 7 get filled against the real repo instead of hypothetically.

## 4. Presentations (~10 min per team)

Each team presents against the same rubric:

| Ask them to show | Listening for |
|------------------|---------------|
| The business problem and capability | Framed as capability, not code |
| The intention and requirements | Traceable, specific, demo-grade minimums named |
| What the agent built or changed | Honest about what the agent got wrong |
| How they verified it | Running software, not the transcript |
| What risks remain | Real ones — auth, data, comprehension debt |
| How their org will use this workflow | The seven-piece kit, filled in, with owners |

The last row is the graded one. A weak demo with a strong adoption plan beats the reverse.

## 5. Send-off

- **The kit goes home in git.** Commit it somewhere the team can see; review changes to it like code.
- **First step Monday:** create the passport file and pick the first low-risk backlog item (kit templates 2 and 7). Everything else follows from there.
- **Staying current:** the themes are stable, the vendor details rotate — re-check plans, prices, and model names quarterly. Optional consulting check-ins exist, but the goal of the week was to make them capable, not dependent.

### What they must leave the course believing

1. Speed is real — a month of loop-latency genuinely becomes a morning — but only the disciplined get it *safely*.
2. Their company's edge is what they encode: intentions, rules, preference skills, evals — not which model they license.
3. Adoption is an operating-model change with named owners, not a tool install.
4. They are ready to start Monday — small, safe, and measured.
