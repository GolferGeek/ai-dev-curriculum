# Course Deliverables: What Participants Leave With

## The Short Version

The course produces more than demo applications. Participants leave with working examples of AI-built software, a repeatable AI development workflow, and a started company-owned [AI Program](ai-program-take-home-product.md): a standard governance/GRC structure, Full/Essential/Light scale profiles, AI-culture and sentiment boundaries, initial organization-specific content, maintained AI capabilities, and an application for navigating and asking questions of the program.

The app is the proof. The process enables delivery. The AI Program is the
durable take-home product the organization continues to build.

## What This Means For A Client

By the end of the course, participants should be able to use modern AI coding agents with structure, discipline, and clear guardrails. They will not simply watch AI generate code. They will practice a full development loop:

```text
idea
-> intention
-> requirements
-> implementation plan
-> agent-assisted build
-> app inspection
-> test and verification
-> review
-> operating-model update
```

That loop is designed for teams that need to build or modify useful business software faster without losing control of quality, security, or human accountability.

## 1. Working Technical Artifacts

Participants leave with concrete examples that prove the workflow works.

Depending on the cohort and selected tracks, these may include:

- A generated monorepo structure.
- One or more working application slices.
- Intention documents.
- Product requirements documents.
- Implementation plans.
- Generated application code.
- Build and test results.
- Browser or app verification evidence.
- Quality scans or review outputs.
- Brownfield codebase research reports.
- Model evaluation results.
- Skill or agent workflow examples.

The Twitter and Facebook product efforts are **not locked to iOS**. Their supplied reference applications demonstrate SwiftUI, but a client may deliver either product intention as a web application instead. The cohort default is web; native iOS is an optional Mac/Xcode specialization.

These artifacts are intentionally practical. They show how a team can move from a business idea to a running software slice, then inspect and verify the result before deciding whether to move on, revise, or harden.

## 2. A Repeatable AI Development Process

The course teaches a repeatable process for using AI development tools responsibly.

Participants learn how to:

- Turn a business idea into a clear intention.
- Convert an intention into requirements.
- Convert requirements into an implementation plan.
- Challenge the intention, requirements, and plan before code is written.
- Start and supervise an AI-assisted build.
- Use build time for higher-value discussion instead of watching code stream by.
- Inspect the generated application.
- Review generated code for scope, correctness, and maintainability.
- Run build, test, browser, and quality checks.
- Compare the final result to the original intention.
- Decide whether the work is acceptable to move on, needs revision, or requires deeper hardening.

The goal is not blind trust in AI. The goal is a disciplined workflow where AI increases cadence and humans remain responsible for intent, risk boundaries, and final judgment.

## 3. Team Operating Model Documents

The most durable course output is a started AI development operating model.

Each team leaves with first drafts of practical documents they can take back to work, refine, and commit to a shared repository. These are deliberately lightweight. The goal is not a giant policy binder. The goal is a usable team operating system.

### Harness And Plan-Tier Decision

Defines:

- Which AI development tools are approved.
- Which subscription or enterprise tiers are acceptable for company work.
- What company data can enter the tool.
- What accounts or personal tools are not allowed for company work.
- Who owns tool and vendor decisions.

### Instruction Passport

Defines:

- Where shared AI instructions live.
- What belongs in `AGENTS.md`, tool rules, skills, or memory.
- What must never live in personal memory.
- How team instructions are reviewed and updated.
- How new team members inherit the same working context.

### Artifact Pipeline

Defines:

- Where intentions, requirements, and plans are stored.
- Who reviews each stage.
- What must be true before an agent starts building.
- What evidence must be saved after a build.
- How the team connects business intent to implementation work.

### Quality Gates

Defines:

- Required build, lint, typecheck, and test steps.
- Required browser or app verification.
- Review expectations for AI-generated changes.
- Security and dependency checks where appropriate.
- What "acceptable to move on" means for the team's work.

### Decision Boundaries

Defines:

- What agents may do without asking.
- What agents must ask before doing.
- What remains human-only.
- Who is accountable for final decisions.
- Where speed is acceptable and where deeper review is required.

### Governance, Risk And Compliance Control Map

Defines:

- Which authoritative organizational, legal, contractual, client, security,
  privacy, regulatory, or audit sources govern the work.
- How data, tools, agent authority, and software changes are classified by risk.
- Which controls, review gates, independent evidence, rollback actions,
  monitoring signals, and stop conditions each tier requires.
- How requirements map to durable repository guidance and auditable evidence.
- How exceptions are requested, approved, bounded, reviewed, expired, and
  revoked.
- Who owns each control, interprets each requirement, approves residual risk,
  and reviews the result.

The course supplies the mapping method and a conservative starting vocabulary.
The client supplies and approves its applicable obligations, risk definitions,
owners, and exception authority. A started map is not a compliance
certification or a declaration that the organization is production-ready.

### Day-2 Repo Safety Rules

Defines:

- How agents may work on the real codebase after the course.
- Which files, systems, or environments are restricted.
- Branch, pull request, and review expectations.
- Secrets and customer-data boundaries.
- Recovery expectations if an agent breaks something.

### Routing And Spend Policy

Defines:

- Which model should be used for which kind of task.
- When a frontier model is worth the cost.
- When a smaller or faster model is enough.
- When parallel agents are worth the burn rate.
- Who monitors quality, cost, and tool usage.

### Skills And Reusable Context Backlog

Defines:

- Which workflows should become repeatable skills.
- Which company standards should become reusable context.
- Which domain or brand preferences should agents inherit.
- Which review patterns should be automated.
- How the team improves its AI harness over time.

## The Course Output In One Sentence

Participants leave with working software, a repeatable AI development workflow, and the first draft of the process documents their team needs to use AI development safely, accountably, and productively.

## Why This Matters

For small and mid-sized teams, AI development changes the economics of the backlog. Work that used to be too slow, too expensive, or too speculative can now be explored quickly.

The value of the course is not just that participants see an AI agent build an app. The value is that they learn how to define the work clearly, guide the agent, inspect the result, verify behavior, and preserve what they learned as team process.

That is what lets a team move from isolated AI experiments to a practical AI development operating model.
